## Theory

A digital color image is typically stored as three 2-D arrays — one for
each of the red, green, and blue channels. A grayscale image stores a
single 2-D array where each value is the perceived brightness of the
corresponding pixel.

### Why not just average the three channels?

Averaging treats R, G, B as equally important. They are not.
Photopic vision peaks in the green region of the spectrum, so a green
pixel and a blue pixel with the same numerical value look very different
in brightness. The luminance formula weights green heavily (0.587) and
blue lightly (0.114) to match human perception.

### Trade-offs

| Method      | Formula                       | Preserves perception? |
|-------------|-------------------------------|-----------------------|
| Luminance   | 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B      | Yes (best)            |
| Average     | (R + G + B) / 3               | No — green too dark   |
| Max channel | max(R, G, B)                  | No — overexposed      |
| Desaturate  | (max + min) / 2               | Partially             |
